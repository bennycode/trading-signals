# Indicator Types

Every indicator in this library can be described along three axes: what it measures, whether it reacts before or after the move, and whether its output is bounded.

## By Function

What the indicator measures. This is how the indicators on this site are grouped:

- [Momentum indicators](/indicators/momentum): Measure the speed and strength (intensity) of price movements in a particular direction (overbought/oversold)
- [Trend indicators](/indicators/trend): Measure the direction of a trend (bullish/bearish)
- [Volatility indicators](/indicators/volatility): Measure the degree of variation in prices over time, regardless of direction
- [Volume indicators](/indicators/volume): Measure the strength of a trend based on volume

**Key readings:**

- Bullish sentiment: expect prices to rise
- Bearish sentiment: expect prices to fall
- Overbought condition: price may have risen too much too fast, meaning it's trending up, but traders expect a short-term dip before continuing higher
- Oversold condition: price may have dropped too much too fast, meaning it's trending down, but traders expect a short-term bounce before continuing lower or reversing upward

## By Timing

Whether the indicator tries to anticipate a move or confirm one that already started:

- **Leading indicators**: Predictive tools that try to signal future price movements before they happen (i.e. [RSI](/indicators/momentum/rsi), [Stochastic Oscillator](/indicators/momentum/stoch), volume spikes)
- **Lagging indicators**: Confirmative tools that signal after a trend or move has already started (i.e. moving averages, [MACD](/indicators/momentum/macd), [ADX](/indicators/trend/adx))

Leading indicators give earlier entries but more false signals; lagging indicators are more reliable but enter later. Many setups combine one of each.

## By Scale

Whether the output is bounded:

- **Indicators**: Have no upper or lower limits
- **Oscillators**: Move within a fixed range (e.g. 0-100, -1 to +1)

Oscillators are what make "overbought" and "oversold" thresholds possible, because a fixed range gives a level to compare against. An unbounded indicator such as a moving average has no such level, so it is read relative to price instead.
